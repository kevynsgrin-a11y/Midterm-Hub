## What "supported" meant, and how it was applied

The audit's "publication-eligible" flag says a claim has two independent, substantively read sources. Before writing anything, every eligible claim was re-read against its cited official source by independent verifiers (six groups, one per slice of states). Each result was one of:

- **CONFIRMED**: an official source states the value, within the claim's scope. Written.
- **PARTIAL**: the date is right but a scope, time, channel or exception differs. Written only with the verifier's corrected scope, never the original wording.
- **CONFLICT** or **UNREADABLE**: not written. The record keeps its previous value and the item is listed under "Residual items" below.

### Mapping into the data model

`ElectionRecord` (`civic/models.py`, `extra="forbid"`) has a small structured surface: `registration_deadline`, `registration_deadline_time` (HH:MM), `early_voting_start`, `early_voting_end`, `mail_ballot_request_deadline`, `candidate_filing_deadline`, `timezone`, `confidence`, `source_url`, `source_retrieved_at`, `notes`. Rules used:

- A date or time went into a structured field only when the audit supported it for the whole jurisdiction and the field's meaning matched.
- Anything channel-specific (mail vs online vs in person), conditional, or scoped (military and overseas voters, particular offices) went into `notes`, which the site displays.
- Contradicted sentences in existing notes were corrected rather than appended to.
- `source_retrieved_at` was set to `2026-10-09T12:00:00Z` only on records whose changed facts were re-read that day. Noon UTC keeps the displayed date correct in every US time zone. Records not re-read keep their old timestamp, so "Retrieved 7/21/2026" is still shown on them. That is intentional: do not relabel unverified claims as newly verified.
- Elections already held carry the word "historical" in `notes` (see the archive gate below).
- Historical primaries for states without a primary record were added as a sentence on the general record. No new primary records were created.

## Where the data lives and how to change it

- Source of truth: `intake/2026-statewide.yaml`. Edit single lines in place and keep the existing double-quoted scalars.
- `generated/elections.json` is a build artifact for the Next.js frontend. Regenerate it with `python -I scripts/build-frontend-data.py`; never edit it by hand. It keeps `generated_at` when nothing changed.
- Before pushing a data change: run the generator (it validates all records with the same Pydantic model), `pytest`, `npm test`, `npm run typecheck`, `npm run lint`, and the legacy renderer pipeline (`civic init`, `civic intake FILE --by editorial --published generated/elections.json`, verify, `civic site build`).

### The 30-day archive gate

`ElectionRecord` rejects a record whose election was more than 30 days ago unless its notes contain the word "historical", or the record is exactly the one already published. Two consumers apply that rule:

- the frontend exporter (`scripts/build-frontend-data.py`), and
- `civic intake`, used by `scripts/build-site.sh` for the GitHub Pages `deploy-site` workflow.

Until PR #25 only the first knew about "already published", so `deploy-site` failed on 2026-10-09 when the NH primary aged out. Both now share `published_key` / `published_index` in `civic/intake.py`. Consequences:

- An unchanged published record stays valid indefinitely; nobody has to edit data when an election ages out (the DE primary crosses 30 days on 2026-10-16, general-election records on 2026-12-04).
- Editing an aged record in any field, provenance timestamp included, requires adding "historical" to its notes.
- The comparison needs the record to be `verified: true` in `generated/elections.json`, so regenerate that file in the same commit as the YAML.

## Residual items from Part 1

Several items could not be verified or were held in the first pass: Ohio (seven claims unreadable), the Louisiana Dec 12 scope, the Nebraska ballot-mailing start, the Hawaii paper deadline, the Montana late-registration end, South Dakota's "no same-day registration", and the New York and Michigan values that came from a single county or city source. They were re-read against other official routes on 2026-10-09: the Ohio Secretary of State's schedule and Revised Code, Louisiana's election-dates page, the Montana Secretary of State's federal calendar, the Hawaii Office of Elections pages, the South Dakota and Michigan statutes, and the New York State Board of Elections pages with Election Law. Only directly confirmed values were published.

### Recheck before Election Day

- **Montana** late registration until 8 p.m. depends on the injunction against SB 490. If it is lifted or reversed the cutoff can move to noon. Recheck the Secretary of State's federal calendar and votemt.gov weekly.
- **Ohio** non-military absentee ballots must now be received by 7:30 p.m. on Election Day (Senate Bill 293, effective 2026-03-20). A court challenge could change that; the Cuyahoga board's homepage banner about a September Supreme Court order concerns USPS ballot-mail rules in another case, not Ohio dates.
- **Hawaii** register-by is the Office of Elections' published Oct 26. Its rule text still says "tenth day".

## Held backlog (Part 2)

The 379 held claims break down as follows (from the audit's own status fields):

| Why held | Claims |
| --- | --- |
| Single source only | 219 |
| Two cited sources, but fewer than two substantive direct reads (indexed-only or inaccessible evidence) | 106 |
| Unverified | 44 |
| Source conflict | 7 |
| Cross-checked scope, derived | 3 |

By field family, 193 concern the general election, 47 absentee or mail rules, 31 primaries, 25 registration, and the rest offices, provenance, runoffs, historical dates and narrative. Delaware (17), Alaska (16), Hawaii (14), Massachusetts (13) and Kansas (12) have the most.

Suggested process, which is the one Part 1 used:

1. Group claims by state so one verifier reads one jurisdiction's official pages end to end.
2. For each claim, require a direct read of an official source for the exact claim and scope, plus a second independent publisher for double-sourcing. Record the verbatim quote and URL. Official pages that return 403 to automated fetches should be retried through another official route (statute text, county election office, the state's PDF calendar); if still unreadable, the claim stays held.
3. Apply only CONFIRMED values, or PARTIAL values restated at their verified scope. Conflicts are written up, not resolved by picking a side.
4. Validate and ship in a small PR per batch, with the verifier output summarized in the PR body.

## Operations notes

- **What serves midtermwatch.com.** The apex and `www` are Cloudflare Worker custom domains for the `midtermwatch-vercel-proxy` Worker, whose upstream is `https://midterm-hub.vercel.app` (the Vercel project; per the repo history its production deployments track `main`). The October 9 data was visible on the live domain once #23 merged. The GitHub Pages `deploy-site` workflow builds a separate legacy Python renderer and is not what the domain serves.
- **`Workers Builds: midterm-hub` is red on every PR and on `main`.** The Cloudflare Worker `midterm-hub` has Workers Builds triggers whose root directory is `/dist`, which does not exist in a fresh clone, so every build stops at the clone step with "root directory not found". The Worker serves no traffic (workers.dev disabled, no routes, no custom domain), and the failure predates the audit. It needs a one-time dashboard change (Workers & Pages, `midterm-hub`, Settings, Builds). The assistant session that investigated had read-only access to Workers Builds, so nothing was changed. Original trigger settings, for reference:
  - "Deploy non-production branches": branches `*` except `claude/civic-calendar-engine-backend-wddfqw`, build `bash scripts/build-site.sh dist`, deploy `npx wrangler versions upload`, root `/dist`. This is the trigger that fires on PRs and on `main`.
  - "Deploy default branch": only the branch `claude/civic-calendar-engine-backend-wddfqw` (dormant), deploy `npx wrangler deploy`, root `/dist`.

  Pausing the first trigger (for example by restricting its branch filter to a name no branch has), or disconnecting the repository from that Worker, ends the red check. Do not simply fix the root directory: that would let the build run `wrangler deploy` for a Worker nobody uses.
- **Tests that depend on today's date.** `tests/conftest.py` builds a fixture site; its VA record carries a "historical" note for the reason above. A fixture with a recent election date will fail once it ages past 30 days unless it carries one too.
